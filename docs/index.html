How this works

Every US-listed common stock is downloaded daily, filtered for tradeable liquidity, then measured against a fixed set of technical setups. Nothing is fitted, learned or optimised — the rules are written down and applied identically to every name.

1. Universe

Symbol directories from NasdaqTrader cover every listing on Nasdaq, NYSE, NYSE American, NYSE Arca, Cboe and IEX. Test issues, ETFs, warrants, units, rights and preferred shares are removed. Names are then dropped unless they trade above $3 with at least $1m of daily dollar volume and have 200 sessions of history — below that, spreads and borrow costs overwhelm any signal.

2. Setups

Each setup has a gate and a score. The gate is a hard requirement: the pattern is either present or it is not, and a name that fails it is never shown at any score. The score, 0–100, measures how clean that instance is across weighted components — relative strength, volume confirmation, trend strength, position within the range. Because every component is normalised before weighting, scores are comparable across different setups.

3. From setup to action

The same pattern justifies different instruments depending on context:

  • Liquidity decides what is executable at all. Options on a stock trading $2m/day are a trap however good the chart is.
  • Implied volatility decides whether you want to buy or sell premium. A strong setup with expensive options is often better expressed as a premium sale than a purchase.
  • Expected speed matters: a squeeze breakout resolves fast, so convexity is worth paying for; a trend pullback grinds for weeks, where a long option just bleeds theta.
  • Borrow risk makes a put structurally safer than shorting a small cap outright — no fee, no recall.
  • Earnings inside the holding period turn a chart bet into a coin flip, and are flagged as such.

4. On the volatility number

A true IV rank needs a year of implied-volatility history, which no free feed provides. What is shown instead is the ratio of at-the-money implied volatility to the volatility the stock has actually delivered, using a gap-resistant estimator so that one merger or FDA announcement doesn't make ordinary options look cheap for months afterwards. Chains that are too thin or too wide-spread to read are marked unreliable and no volatility-based suggestion is made for them.

5. Levels

Stops sit 2 ATR from entry and targets 3.5 ATR, so risk is scaled to each stock's own volatility rather than a flat percentage. Anything whose resulting reward-to-risk falls below 1.3 is discarded. These are mechanical reference levels, not instructions.

What this cannot do

It has no knowledge of fundamentals, news, filings, guidance, litigation, dilution or anything else that actually moves stocks. It cannot tell you position size. It has not been backtested, and a matched pattern is not an edge. Treat every card as the start of your own research.
